# 👤 Student Profile — Ethan

Back to [[00 Index]] · See also [[Teaching Playbook]], [[Common Mistakes]]

## Basics
- **University:** University of Twente — Calculus A
- **Level:** first time learning calculus in depth
- **Languages:** English + Bahasa Indonesia (Indonesian welcome for hard concepts; uses words like *turunan*, *pembilang*, *penyebut*)
- **Background:** design, fabrication, embedded/IoT development → responds well to engineering / physical analogies

## How Ethan learns best
- ✅ Very detailed, step-by-step — no skipped steps
- ✅ The **why** at every step, not just the how
- ✅ Name the rule explicitly ("this is the Chain Rule")
- ✅ Several worked examples per concept (3–5)
- ✅ Numerical verification — trusts numbers more than pure algebra
- ✅ Analogies and visual breakdowns
- ✅ Let Ethan identify inner/outer layers himself before solving
- ✅ **"Gogo gaga" mode — Ethan's own words, 2026-09-18.** Explain as if he is a complete beginner who has never seen the notation. Baby-talk restatement of every formula in plain words, one idea per step, name the trap before the method. This is now the **default** register for every explanation, not something he has to ask for. Full recipe in [[Teaching Playbook]].
- ❌ Rushing, assuming prior knowledge, abstract-only explanations
- ❌ **Raw LaTeX in chat.** Ethan reads the chat in a terminal, where `$\dfrac{a}{b}$` does **not** render — it is unreadable to him. In conversation write plain text: `a/b`, `s^(-3/2)`, `sqrt(2)`, `integral from 1 to sqrt(2) of ... ds`. LaTeX stays in these Obsidian notes only, because Obsidian renders it.

## Personality
Thoughtful, asks "why", honest when lost, catches errors when shown a numerical check, and will catch the tutor's mistakes too. Follow-up questions are a good sign — lean into them.

## Current state (as of 2026-09-16)
**Solid:** derivative definition, all differentiation rules, implicit differentiation, inverse-function derivative, extrema on closed intervals, sign charts, L'Hôpital (incl. repeated), basic antiderivatives.

**Still building:**
- Algebra simplification (fractions, what may be cancelled)
- Minus signs in trig derivatives / antiderivatives
- Quickly spotting layers in multi-layer compositions
- Checking the domain **first** (roots, logs, denominators)
- Antiderivative patterns becoming automatic
- **Choosing** the technique (jumped to Chain Rule on a fraction problem, 2026-09-16)
- Special angle values — now understands them from the unit circle rather than memorised
